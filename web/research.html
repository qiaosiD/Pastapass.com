Research write-up

Is a bot faster than a finger — and by how much, really?

Four hypotheses, one mock drop, one monotonic clock. Every arm is run repeatedly and its distribution — not a single number — is what gets judged. Latencies are right-skewed, so the summary below leans on the honest tools: percentiles, a bootstrap 95% CI on the median, and nonparametric Mann–Whitney U + Cliff's δ to decide whether an arm is really faster or just lucky.

The thesis, in one breath

You can't beat the speed of light down a wire, so raw click-speed is a myth — the win comes from being ready: skip the browser, warm the socket, and fire on a synced clock rather than reacting to the signal. Each of those is a testable claim below, and each one holds — the strategy dominates, while the programming language barely matters.

The levers — ranked by what they cost you

The loopback numbers isolate the sub-millisecond floor. On a real target these are the levers that actually decide it — biggest cost first. Nail the top ones; the language is genuinely last.

LeverCost if you get it wrongThe fix — and how to push further
1Clock accuracy Unbounded

Fire at the wrong instant and nothing else matters.

  • Sync to the server's clock — sample a time endpoint, compute the offset NTP-style (round-trip midpoint), and schedule the fire against its launch instant, not your wall clock.
  • Discipline the local clock with chrony/PTP and fire a hair early, keeping a reactive long-poll as a backstop — first success wins.
2Browser overhead Tens–hundreds of ms

Skip the DOM entirely on the firing path.

  • Reverse-engineer the buy request and fire raw HTTP; keep a browser only to mint the session/cookies, never to click.
  • If a browser is mandatory (anti-bot), inject fetch() from in-page JS and pre-capture the token so firing is one request, not render→observe→click.
3Cold connect ~95 ms

Warm socket. Non-negotiable.

  • Pre-open a keep-alive TCP+TLS connection to the exact endpoint minutes ahead, with periodic keepalives so it isn't reaped.
  • Use TLS 1.3 session resumption / 0-RTT early data and HTTP/2 reuse, and pre-serialize the request bytes so the fire is a single write().
4Distance to their server 50–80 ms

Run from a cloud region physically near their origin — same region drops the round-trip to 1–5 ms.

  • Fingerprint the origin's provider/region (traceroute, TLS SNI, response headers) and deploy the bot in that exact region.
  • Pre-resolve and pin the origin IP to skip DNS at fire time, and target the origin directly rather than a distant CDN edge for the write.
5Detection lag 5–50+ ms

Proactive instead of polling.

  • Fire proactively at the synced launch instant (retry if the server flips late), with a reactive long-poll fallback.
  • Replace short-interval polling with a held-open channel (long-poll / SSE / WebSocket) so you learn of the release in one hop, not on the next tick.
6Language ~2 ms

Last. Genuinely last.

  • Fix every lever above first; only then swap to a compiled runtime (Rust/Go) with an allocation-free, pre-warmed hot path.
  • Pin the process to a core, raise scheduling priority, and warm the fire path once before the drop so the real fire pays no cold-start.

Costs are typical real-world magnitudes on a live target — orders of magnitude above the loopback floor measured below.

How the numbers are made

  • Percentiles by linear interpolation (type-7), so p50/p90/p95/p99 are comparable to numpy's defaults.
  • Median 95% CI by seeded nonparametric bootstrap (2,000 resamples) — no normality assumed, and reproducible from the commit.
  • Between-arm test: Mann–Whitney U (two-sided, tie- and continuity-corrected). It asks "are these two distributions different?" without assuming a bell curve.
  • Effect size: Cliff's δ — how big the difference is, independent of n, with negligible/small/medium/large bands.
  • Honest caveats: runs are on loopback (network RTT would dominate a real target but the ranking holds), n varies per arm (browser arms are slower to sample, so their CIs are wider), and nothing here touches a real store.
